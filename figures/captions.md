# Figure captions (draft)

All figures are generated by `pipeline/10_make_figures.py` from the saved `results/` files; the exact
plotted numbers are in the matching `*_data.csv`. Gap = (achieved / best-known optimum - 1) in %, over
the contexts with a feasible design; lower is better; 0 matches the best-known reference. The
reference is the best known, not a proven optimum: differential evolution at 4,800 evaluations lands
slightly below it. "Evaluations" count EC3 analyses per design, including those of the post-hoc
operators. Headline operator mode is `scale+thin` unless stated.

**Fig. 0. Study overview.** A context (span and factored UDL) is mapped to a design by an amortized
policy (PPO, trained once without labels), by a kNN over solved contexts, or by a per-context search
(GA, DE, random). Every design passes the same EC3 check and the same post-hoc operators and is
scored against the pooled best-known reference for the same objective (cost, mass or CO2) on the main
grid and on the out-of-distribution (OOD) grids.

**Fig. 1. Quality versus evaluations per design on the main grid (142 contexts).** Per-context
search (GA, DE, random; budgets B = 20 ... 4800; lines are means over 5 seeds, shading is ± sd) against
PPO (mean ± sd over 5 seeds) and the kNN with pooled labels (leave-one-out, k = 3; deterministic).
Vertical axis is symmetric-logarithmic. At about 100 evaluations the policy is far ahead of the
searches; GA and DE need roughly 450-1,000 evaluations to reach its gap and beat it at B = 4,800; the
kNN is ahead of all methods at fewer evaluations but needs solved contexts beforehand (Fig. 4).

**Fig. 2. In distribution versus out of distribution.** Top: gap; bottom: share of feasible designs,
for PPO (mean ± sd over 5 seeds) and the kNN with pooled labels (trained on the 142 grid contexts;
leave-one-out for the in-distribution column). OOD sets contain only contexts for which a feasible
reference design exists (26 of 64 for OOD span, 47 of 64 for OOD load, cost objective). The joint
extrapolation set has no feasible reference and is not evaluated (Fig. 6). The post-hoc operators
contribute much of the OOD gap (Fig. 5).

**Fig. 3. Ablations on the main grid, cost objective.** Per-seed gaps (dots) and mean (bar). (a) PPO
reward modes: no pairwise difference survives Holm correction (n = 5 per arm). (b) PPO versus SAC,
TD3 and DDPG: only PPO versus TD3 is significant after Holm correction (p = 0.048); the off-policy
algorithms are unstable across seeds (SAC and DDPG each contain seeds near PPO's range, one DDPG seed
is at 88%).

**Fig. 4. kNN gap versus labelling cost, in distribution.** kNN trained on n = 36, 72 or 108 labelled
contexts (subsample protocol, 10 repetitions) with labels from one DE search per context (budget B =
400, 1,000 or 4,800; mean ± sd over 3 label seeds) or from the pooled best-known designs (about
96,000 evaluations per context). Labelling cost is n x B. PPO is shown at its 1,000,000 training
steps with its mean gap as a horizontal line. The comparison is of training/labelling cost only: the
kNN needs labelled contexts and RL does not.

**Fig. 5. Contribution of the post-hoc operators.** Gap over feasible designs (top, symmetric-logarithmic) and share of feasible designs (bottom) under no operator, `scale` and `scale+thin`, for PPO (mean over 5 seeds), the kNN (leave-one-out, pooled labels) and searches with budget B = 40. Evaluations differ between methods and operator modes (for example PPO without operator is the best of about 40 policy steps, the kNN one prediction). The operators supply most of the feasibility of the kNN and the searches and a large part of the quality of every method; at equal evaluations PPO has the lower gap than the searches in all nine (objective, method) comparisons with `scale+thin` and in seven of nine without any operator (Table 10).

**Fig. 6. Reference landscape.** Cost-, mass- and CO2-optimal grade (colour) of every context of the
main grid and the three OOD grids; crosses mark contexts without a feasible design. The dashed box is
the training range. Cost favours S355 almost everywhere, mass mixes grades, and CO2 is S690 for every
feasible context; welded sections are optimal for a few mass and many CO2 contexts at low loads. The
joint-extrapolation grid has no feasible design.

**Fig. 7 (supplementary). Training curves.** Mean training-episode reward per seed (thin lines) of PPO for
the cost, mass and CO2 objectives (a); training reward (b) and episode length (c) of PPO, SAC, TD3 and DDPG in
the algorithm comparison; training reward of the shaped (d) and Lagrangian (e) reward modes; mean constraint
violation (f) and Lagrange multiplier (g) per constraint, averaged over seeds, for the Lagrangian runs. Rewards
are on the scale of their reward mode and are not comparable between panels (d), (e) and (a)-(b); the
comparison metric of the paper is the gap of Figs. 1-3. In (d) the shaped reward is still rising at 1M steps in all five seeds. In (c) the off-policy
seeds end with different episode lengths (SAC about 8 steps in two seeds and 17-18 in two others), PPO stays near 35-39.
